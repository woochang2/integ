\begin{abstract}
DAG-based BFT protocols decouple transaction dissemination from ordering. A critical design choice is the \emph{cut rule}: how the consensus leader selects disseminated data for each commit. We compare two strategies: the \emph{conservative cut} of Relaxed Mempool (top $2f{+}1$ validators, uniform $H_{\min}$) and the \emph{inclusive cut} of Autobahn (all $n$ lane tips). Through experiments on a 10-validator LAN cluster, we show the conservative cut achieves 42\% lower latency under stragglers, while the inclusive cut recovers 10$\times$ faster from network blips. We further propose and implement an \emph{adaptive cut} that dynamically switches between both strategies based on validator height disparity, achieving the best of both worlds across all conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\vspace{-0.3em}

DAG-based BFT protocols~\cite{narwhal,bullshark,autobahn} achieve high throughput by separating data dissemination from ordering. Validators broadcast batches into a DAG; a consensus layer periodically \emph{cuts} the DAG to finalize transactions. While consensus algorithms have been extensively studied, the \textbf{cut rule}---how the leader decides \emph{which} portion of the DAG to commit---has received less attention. Yet this choice determines straggler resilience, fairness, and the \emph{hangover effect}~\cite{autobahn} (post-blip recovery speed).

We compare two cut strategies from Relaxed Mempool~\cite{relaxed} and Autobahn~\cite{autobahn}, identify their complementary strengths, and propose an \textbf{adaptive cut} that combines both. All three are implemented on a shared Narwhal-based framework.

\vspace{-0.5em}
\section{Cut Rule Design Space}
\vspace{-0.3em}

Both systems use \textbf{weak quorums ($f{+}1$)} for speculative data dissemination. The key difference is how the leader constructs commit proposals.

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent\textbf{Conservative Cut (Relaxed Mempool).} Each block carries a \emph{stream height list}---a vector of known heights per validator. The leader: (1) sorts validators by height, (2) selects the top $2f{+}1$, (3) computes $H_{\min}$ = min of their heights, and (4) commits all blocks up to $H_{\min}$. This \textbf{uniform cut} excludes the bottom $f$ validators, preventing stragglers from delaying commits.

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent\textbf{Inclusive Cut (Autobahn).} Each validator runs an independent \emph{lane}. The leader proposes $n$ certified tip references---one per lane. Lane coverage requires ${\geq}n{-}f$ new tips. After commit, replicas sync and interleave. This \textbf{per-lane cut} includes all validators at their own position. No validator is excluded.

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent\textbf{Adaptive Cut (Our Proposal).} We observe that the two strategies have complementary strengths: conservative excels under persistent stragglers; inclusive excels during blip recovery. We propose an adaptive cut that monitors the \emph{height disparity} (max $-$ min validator height in the DAG). When disparity exceeds a threshold $\delta$ (indicating stragglers), the engine uses the conservative cut. When disparity drops below $\delta$ (nodes converging, e.g., post-blip), it switches to the inclusive cut for rapid backlog recovery.

\noindent\textbf{Straggler scenario} (Fig.~\ref{fig:straggler}): With 4 stragglers (+300\,ms delay), the conservative cut reaches 170K\,tx/s at 1.2\,s by excluding slow validators. The inclusive cut achieves 160K\,tx/s at 1.5\,s (sync overhead from slow lanes). The \textbf{adaptive cut matches the conservative cut} (172K\,tx/s at 1.18\,s) because high height disparity triggers Relaxed mode.

\noindent\textbf{Hangover effect} (Fig.~\ref{fig:hangover}): After a 5s blip, the conservative cut takes $\sim$20s to recover ($H_{\min}$ advances gradually). The inclusive cut recovers in $\sim$2s (one slot commits all tips). The \textbf{adaptive cut recovers in $\sim$2.5s}---as the blip ends, height disparity drops below $\delta$, triggering the switch to Inclusive mode for rapid backlog commit.

\noindent\textbf{Happy path}: Without stragglers, all four engines perform comparably ($\sim$195--205K\,tx/s, $\sim$500\,ms).

\section{Conclusion}
\vspace{-0.3em}

The cut rule is a first-class design dimension in DAG-based BFT. Conservative cuts (Relaxed Mempool) and inclusive cuts (Autobahn) represent two ends of a spectrum, each optimal under different conditions. Our adaptive cut demonstrates that these strategies can be unified: by monitoring height disparity at runtime, the system dynamically selects the optimal mode---achieving low straggler latency \emph{and} seamless blip recovery. Both strategies share the same DAG substrate, making the runtime switch zero-overhead.
